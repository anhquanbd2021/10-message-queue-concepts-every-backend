Queue Guarantees Lab · mechanics

Mechanics guide

Queue Guarantees Lab models a toy broker: a partitioned append-only log and consumer groups that each keep a durable committed offset. Everything the labs show falls out of two facts — delivery and commit are separate events, and every group owns its own bookmark.

Delivery lab — at-least-once is a trade, not a feature

poll() delivers records from the committed offset but moves no bookmark. commit() moves it — only after the handler's work. The crash lands between the two: the charge applied, the bookmark didn't. On restart the consumer resumes from the committed offset and the tail is redelivered. That is at-least-once delivery — duplicates are the price of not losing.

Naive vs idempotent: the naive handler applies the effect on every delivery — the crashed record's charge applies twice (11 effects for 10 events). The idempotent consumer keeps a dedupe store keyed on partition:offset (a real system prefers a business key like a payment id); the redelivery is seen, acknowledged, and skipped — 10 effects.

Dead-letter lab — quarantine, not a trash can

recordFailure() counts attempts. Below maxAttempts the record stays uncommitted, so the next poll() redelivers it — the retry. At the cap it is parked: pushed to the DLQ and committed past, so one poison message can't hold the partition hostage while job-5 waits forever.

What belongs: messages that can never succeed — corrupt payloads, schema mismatches. Transient failures (a downstream timeout) deserve backoff retries, not the DLQ. And a DLQ nobody drains is a graveyard — drainDlq() hands parked records back for replay or audit.

Backpressure lab — depth is the metric

Each tick produces producerRate and consumes consumerRate; the queue absorbs the difference. Depth grows producer − consumer per tick, so a 5-vs-3 gap adds 2/tick: the alert line (15) is crossed at tick 7, capacity (20) shortly after. This model's overflow policy blocks the producer — real systems also drop, spill, or autoscale, but every policy is a choice someone made or a default someone didn't.

Recovery: stall the producer at tick 8 and depth still falls only at consumer rate — a 16-deep backlog at 3/tick takes ~5 more ticks to drain. Queue depth (consumer lag) is the alert that sees this coming; "service is up" never does.

Fan-out lab — every group keeps its own bookmark

billing and analytics are two groups on one topic. Each has a private committed-offset map, so each sees every record — one write, many independent reads. Crash billing mid-read and only billing redelivers; rewind billing and it replays history while analytics never notices. A second consumer in the same group would split the work instead of copying it — group membership decides which semantics you get.

Ordering lives here too: the partition lanes show the contract — hash(key) mod partitions pins every customer-c3 event to one lane, in produce order. There is no global order across lanes, and keyless events scatter to whichever partition the cursor points at.

Honest limits

  • This is an in-memory model of the mechanics — not a broker. No network, no real protocol, no persistence.
  • Crashes are injected deterministically between work and commit; real crashes land anywhere — the dedupe store must cover every record, not just suspicious ones.
  • One member per group; real groups assign partitions across members and rebalance on joins/leaves.
  • The backpressure model picks one overflow policy (block); real brokers also drop, page to disk, or push back on producers with quotas.
  • The dedupe store is an in-memory Set; production needs durable storage with a retention window longer than the redelivery horizon.
